Passphrases

The passphrase you come up with is used to encrypt and secure your OnlyLogins account. The data in your account is not possible to decrypt without your passphrase, so it important to make it both strong and easy to remember.

OnlyLogins requires a passphrase of at least 16 characters. Long passphrases made from several unrelated words are easier to remember and much harder to guess than short passwords with lots of symbols. The classic XKCD comic correct horse battery staple example is a good model to follow. Avoid common phrases, song lyrics, quotes, and long runs of repeated or sequential characters such as aaaaa or 12345.

The 16-character minimum comes from the United States National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Their digital identity guidelines, SP 800-63B, recommend a minimum of 15 characters for a passphrase that is the only factor protecting an account. OnlyLogins follows that recommendation and adds one more character for good measure. On top of the length requirement, your passphrase must also contain at least 80 bits of entropy, which ensures a long passphrase is not just padded with repeated or predictable characters.

Use a passphrase that is unique to OnlyLogins. Do not reuse a passphrase from another website and do not store it in email, chat, or any another online account. There is no recovery mechanism. If you forget your passphrase, no one, including OnlyLogins support, can restore your data. Consider keeping a written copy offline in a secure physical safe.

Secure Documents

A document is a single secure record for one account, website, or service. For example you might create one for your email login, one for your electric service provider, one for your twitter. The contents of each document are encrypted using the secure passphrase used to log into OnlyLogins.

Each document lives inside a folder so you can group related things together. Inside a document you can store:

  • link(s) to the website
  • username(s)
  • password(s)
  • key->value pairs (for security questions, recovery codes, service keys)
  • a secure note (for any additional information)

Most users create one document per website or per service. Pick a short, recognizable name for the document. Simply using the website or service name is usually a good choice (for example amazon, netflix, or twitter).

Folders

A folder is used to group together common documents. You might create a "socials" folder to keep Twitter, Facebook, Reddit documents in one place, for example. You may create as many folders as you would like, and each folder may contain any number of documents.

Your account comes with a "miscellaneous" by default to help you get started.

Password Generators

Strong passwords need to be unique, long, and random. Your web browser such as Chrome, Safari, Firefox has a built-in password generator that is capable of generating very secure passwords. It is the most ideal option for creating new login credentials.

When you add a password to a document, your web browser will offer to generate a strong random password for you. Look for the key icon or small popup that appears in or near the password field.
